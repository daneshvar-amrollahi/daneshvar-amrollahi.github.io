Happiness is just a distraction

2025/04/06

I’ve recently come to the conclusion that life is, by default, taking us toward destruction. A great piece of evidence for this is death—everyone dies in the end. There are many similar examples around us: plants die, our electronic devices eventually stop working like they once did, food goes bad and starts to smell, and we become physically less capable as we age.

All we do in life is ignore this fact and try to delay the destruction we’re all headed for. These efforts are what keep us sane—what we call happiness. Yet, happiness isn’t some ultimate state; it’s merely our way of escaping the fact that, in the end, it’s all about destruction.

For instance, when you stop working out, your body slowly loses the muscle you’ve built over the weeks. Likewise, if you stop challenging your mind by learning new things, it becomes increasingly difficult to learn later. The only thing we can do is deliberately put ourselves in challenging situations to delay that inevitable decay; otherwise, the process accelerates.
